# Invariant Labs Is Now an Official ETH Zurich Spin-Off

**Source:** Invariant Labs Blog  
**Date:** August 12, 2024  
**Author:** Marc Fischer  
**Tags:** ETH Zurich, spin-off, research, founding, AI security

---

## From Research to Company

Invariant Labs has achieved formal recognition as an ETH Zurich spin-off. The designation reflects the company's origins in multi-year research conducted by its founding team at ETH Zurich — work that began as academic investigation into securing and controlling AI systems and has since translated into a commercially deployed product.

The ETH spin-off programme recognises companies that are directly founded on research produced at the institution. It's a marker of scientific rigour rather than an operational change — but it carries weight in research-adjacent communities as a signal about the depth of technical foundations.

---

## The Research That Became Invariant

The foundational work addressed a question that, in 2022 and 2023, was still largely theoretical: how do you provide formal guarantees about what an AI system will and won't do?

That research was published at top-tier venues — NeurIPS, ICML — and received recognition from:

- **NIST** (National Institute of Standards and Technology)
- **The Future of Privacy Forum**
- Coverage in WIRED, MIT Technology Review, and NZZ

The academic attention predated the current commercial interest in AI safety by several years. When LLM-based agents became practically deployable in 2023, the team had a head start: existing formalisms for constraining AI behaviour, an approach to policy specification, and empirical results showing where current safety measures fail.

---

## The Direction

The company's focus from the start has been on AI agent security — specifically on providing infrastructure that makes it possible to deploy agentic systems in production without accepting an opaque threat model.

The ETH heritage shows up in the product design: Invariant's approach to guardrailing uses formal policy specification rather than prompt-based guidance, because the academic grounding is in systems that can be verified rather than systems that rely on probabilistic model behaviour.

---

## Why the Spin-Off Status Matters

For a company working on the intersection of AI capability and AI safety, credibility in the research community matters. Guardrails, agent testing frameworks, and security benchmarks need to be trusted not just by enterprise buyers but by researchers who will scrutinise the technical claims.

The ETH spin-off designation, combined with publications at NeurIPS and ICML and an active research agenda (AgentDojo, the CTF series, formal security guarantees), positions Invariant as a research-backed company rather than a security vendor making claims about AI it doesn't deeply understand.
